## What True Beef is

A traceability platform for premium US beef. Each animal is anchored to a **biometric
muzzle-print identity**, its life history is written to a **shared immutable ledger**,
certifying bodies attach **audit attestations**, processors attach a **QR code at packing**,
and a consumer **scans the QR at point of sale** to see the farm, diet, and certification
behind their cut. Initial scope is deliberately narrow: the overlap of two claims with
real commercial/legal weight right now — **grass-fed** and **halal**.

## The central design constraint (read this before proposing anything)

Prior blockchain food-traceability efforts (notably IBM Food Trust) stalled in the US because
**farmers were asked to do extra data entry for a benefit that accrued to retailers.** True
Beef treats this as the primary constraint, not a footnote:

- The farm app must be a **herd-management tool first**; traceability is a byproduct, never the pitch.
- Muzzle capture happens **during existing handling events** (weaning, vaccination, weighing) — never as a separate chute pass.
- Fields (birth date, breed, sire/dam, diet) **auto-populate/carry forward** to minimize repeat typing.
- **Cost of participation for the farm is near zero** — licensing fees bill to certifying bodies and retailers, not farms.

If a feature adds farm-side work without farm-side value, it is wrong by default.

## Architecture — four-stage data flow

```
Farm capture ──► Immutable ledger ──► Certifier attestations ──► Processor QR ──► Consumer scan
(muzzle print,     (per-animal,          (audit trail,            (attach QR      (public
 birth, diet)       biometric-anchored)   white-labeled)           at packing)     provenance page)
```

- **Ledger is per-individual-animal and biometric-anchored** — this is the differentiator. Competitors are lot/herd-level only. Do not collapse to lot-level except as an explicit processor fallback mode (Persona B).
- **Ledger schema must be certification-agnostic** — federal grass-fed / "Product of USA" rules have changed repeatedly since 2016; new standards must be absorbable without a rebuild.
- **Immutability matters** — once an inspection is logged it must not be silently alterable (core certifier trust requirement).
- Certification-licensing revenue attaches at the **ledger/attestation layer**; data-insights SaaS draws from the **aggregate ledger** and must never expose an individual farm's raw records or a consumer-facing record without consent.

## Personas drive requirements (v1)

- **A – Rancher**: offline-first mobile app w/ sync (rural low-signal); capture inside handling events; one-tap export formatted for AGA / USDA SVS / halal auditors; herd-benchmark dashboard as the retention hook.
- **B – Processor / halal abattoir**: QR generated by scanning the animal's existing muzzle/ledger ID at intake (no re-entry); certifying-authority attestation captured at slaughter; **batch/lot fallback mode** to lower adoption barrier.
- **C – Certifying body (AGA-style / halal authority)**: auditor portal; immutable audit trail; **white-label** — their logo/standards stay the consumer trust mark, platform is infrastructure, not a competing certifier.
- **D – Retailer/brand (Whole Foods tier)**: API/dashboard to verify supplier certification before listing; per-product QR fee billed to the brand.
- **E – Consumer**: QR resolves in **< 2s** to one mobile page (farm name/location/photo, cert badge + body + audit date, halal chain-of-custody, birth-to-shelf timeline); **no login**; deeper detail (breed, diet, genealogy) only behind one extra tap, never shown by default.
- **F – Data buyer (insurer/researcher)**: insurance-grade identity-verification API (IFFCO-Tokio model); anonymized aggregated benchmarking feed.

## Non-goals (v1)

- Not a new competing certifier — platform is the record-keeping/verification **backend**.
- No commodity/grain-fed beef until the premium segment is proven.
- **US-born, US-raised animals only** — no international supply chains in v1.

## Monetization (shapes the data model)

1. **Certification licensing** — (A) infrastructure licensing to certifying bodies at ~$0.75–1.25/animal billed to the body; (B) per-QR/per-SKU verification fee to retailers ~$0.10–0.30/unit. Faster path to revenue (sells into existing compliance budgets).
2. **Data insights SaaS** — producer benchmarking ($25–75/mo), insurer/anti-fraud API, market-intelligence subscription ($500–2,000/mo). Higher-margin but only viable once enough animals are on the ledger (~18–24 months in).

## Roadmap phasing (build order)

1. **Farm layer** (mo 1–4) — capture app, offline sync, muzzle-ID pipeline, diet/birth logging → exit: 10 pilot ranches logging.
2. **Certification bridge** (mo 3–6) — integrate 1 AGA-style + 1 halal authority as backend → exit: 1 body live.
3. **Processing & QR** (mo 5–8) — QR assignment at small halal/vertically-integrated processors → exit: first shelf product with live QR.
4. **Consumer layer** (mo 6–9) — scan experience + retailer verification API → exit: 1 retail/D2C partner live.
5. **Data insights SaaS** (mo 18–24).

Build in this order. The farm layer is the foundation and the adoption risk; don't skip ahead to consumer/SaaS features before the ledger has real animals in it.

## Technical notes

- **Muzzle-print CV is not the bottleneck** — validated at 98.7–100% accuracy (incl. a US feedlot dataset; few-shot approaches need only 5–7 images/animal). The hard problem is **field capture discipline**: getting a clean image at the right handling moment across thousands of farms. Design around capture UX and robustness to soiled muzzles (Stoktake precedent), not model accuracy.
